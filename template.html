§ 01 · Market layer

Where the whitespace is

The whitespace lives between the two poles of {sector} software: horizontal ERPs that are feature-rich but generic, and single-purpose tools that solve one job but ignore everything around it. The map below is the working set — the gaps are ranked by how far a buyer will travel to close them.

North pole
Horizontal ERPs

Feature-complete, generic, priced for the enterprise. They ship the module nobody in {sector} actually uses, and under-invest in the workflows that define the day.

South pole
Single-purpose tools

Deep on one job, shallow on everything else. The buyer owns 4–6 of them and spends their week in the gaps between.

The whitespace
The integrated middle

A coherent workflow across 3–4 jobs, at a price between the two poles. No incumbent owns this position today — that is the opportunity.

§ 03 · Build pipeline

Sequencing the first 12 months

The pipeline orders the cards by build cost and trigger strength, not by size. The early stages are cheap to prove and expensive to be wrong about, so they go first. Each stage has a kill-criterion: if the metric doesn't move, stop and re-rank.

STAGE 01

Prove the trigger

Land the 5 design partners who feel the gap today. Not a demo — a working session on their real data.

Kill: < 3 of 5 sign
STAGE 02

Ship the wedge

One job, done better than the incumbent's equivalent. The buyer should be able to retire a tab in their browser.

Kill: retention < 70% week 4
STAGE 03

Wire the integration

The data flow that makes the wedge stick: the one that turns "nice tool" into "the system".

Kill: NPS < 30 at 3 mo
STAGE 04

Expand the middle

Add the adjacent job the buyer already owns a single-purpose tool for. Price up, not down.

Kill: attach < 2 jobs/user
